QR settings

What do M, Q, and H mean?

M (~15%) is the recommended default and usually gives the best balance between QR density and damage tolerance. Q (~25%) adds redundancy for wear or defects, but can make the QR denser. H (~30%) has the most redundancy and is usually the densest. Higher error correction is not automatically easier to scan at the same physical size.

Common Cents (FAQ)

A few cents-ible answers before you make a batch.

Does a Contact QR need the internet?

No. The contact data is encoded directly in the QR. A compatible scanner can interpret it without fetching a hosted contact file.

Are Contact and Basic QRs tracked?

No. They are generated directly in the browser and do not route a scan through the tracking service.

What makes the Trackable option different?

The QR contains a redirect URL. When scanned, that redirect can log the scan before sending the visitor to the destination. It therefore requires an online service.

What do M, Q, and H change?

They change error-correction redundancy. More redundancy can improve tolerance to damage but can also increase QR density. M is the default balance.

Why did my QR become more complicated?

More encoded information can require a larger QR matrix. Shortening optional content can sometimes reduce the QR version.

How small can I make it?

There is no single safe physical minimum. Module size, contrast, reproduction method, material, lighting, and scanner quality all affect reliability. Use the estimate here as a starting point and test the final artifact.

Square or circle?

Square is the conventional QR geometry. Circular styling is decorative and should be tested particularly carefully before producing multiples.

The cents-ible rule: generate it, reproduce it at final size, then scan-test it before making a batch.
